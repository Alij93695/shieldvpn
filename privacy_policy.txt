Privacy Policy for ShieldVPN

Effective Date: 23 September 2026
Last Updated: 23 September 2026

1. Introduction

ShieldVPN ("the app") is a free VPN client for Android that uses the OpenVPN protocol. This
policy explains what the app does with your information, and — just as importantly — what
it cannot control.

Please read section 3 carefully. ShieldVPN does not operate its own VPN servers.

2. Information ShieldVPN Collects

ShieldVPN does not operate any backend servers and does not have user accounts. The app
does not collect, store, or transmit:
- Your browsing history
- The contents of your traffic
- Your IP address or DNS queries (except as the network requests below necessarily reveal it)
- Any personally identifying information

The app stores your selected region on your own device only. There is no ShieldVPN server
to send anything to.

The app makes three kinds of network request on its own behalf. Like any web request, each
one reveals your IP address to the host it contacts. None carries personal information, and
all of them happen outside the VPN tunnel.

Server list: the app downloads the server directory from a public community mirror hosted
on GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com, with jsDelivr as a fallback), falling back to
vpngate.net. The request carries no identifier.

Server checks: when you tap Connect, the app contacts a sample of candidate VPN servers
directly (up to about 50 at a time) to measure which ones are reachable and fastest from
your network. Each check opens a connection to the server and closes it without logging in
or sending any of your data. The operators of those servers can see that your IP address
connected.

Update checks: once per launch, and only after you have accepted the disclosure, the app
asks Expo's update service (u.expo.dev) whether a new version is available. That request
carries a random installation identifier along with your platform and app version, and is
handled under Expo's privacy policy.

3. Third-Party VPN Servers (Important)

ShieldVPN does not run its own VPN infrastructure. It connects you to free, volunteer-run
servers published by the VPNGate project, an academic experiment operated by the University
of Tsukuba, Japan (https://www.vpngate.net/).

This means:
- Your traffic is carried by volunteer server operators who are unknown to us and whom we do
  not control, audit, or have any agreement with.
- The VPNGate project states that it retains connection logs (including source IP addresses
  and connection timestamps) for a period of time, and that logs may be disclosed to
  authorities where legally required. See VPNGate's own policy for current details.
- Individual volunteer operators may independently log or inspect traffic passing through
  their servers.

ShieldVPN therefore CANNOT and DOES NOT promise a "no-logs" VPN service. Any such claim
would be false. Do not use ShieldVPN for traffic where interception by an unknown third
party would harm you.

Always use HTTPS. A VPN tunnel protects traffic between your device and the volunteer
server; it does not protect traffic beyond that server.

4. DNS

By default the app directs DNS queries inside the tunnel to public resolvers (Cloudflare
1.1.1.1 and Google 8.8.8.8). Those providers operate under their own privacy policies.

5. Permissions

- VPN (BIND_VPN_SERVICE): required to create the encrypted tunnel.
- Notifications: required by Android to show the ongoing connection status while the VPN
  is active.
- Network state: required to detect connectivity changes and reconnect.

6. Encryption

Connections use the OpenVPN protocol. Because the app connects to community servers, the
available cipher suites are determined by each server operator and may include older
configurations.

7. Children

ShieldVPN is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect information
from them.
